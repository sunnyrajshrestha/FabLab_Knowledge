Before you touch anything

The golden rules of Room 408

1

Tap the dog, every visit

Sign in each time by tapping your phone on the NFC tag on our mascot dog's collar by the door.

2

Scan before you use

Log every session on the 3D printer, laser, 3D scanner, power tools and wet lab via the machine's QR sticker.

3

Request form for risky work

Bandsaw, drilling and power tools need a request form in advance, approved and supervised in person by your professor, plus Sunny.

4

Tell us 2 days ahead

For other project work, email or LINE Sunny at least 2 days ahead (1 day minimum), during working hours.

5

Makers only

The lab is not a meeting, Zoom or study room for work unrelated to the FabLab.

6

Never guess, ask

Unsure about a material, setting or tool? Stop and ask. Guessing breaks machines and people.

Dress for the machine

Our dress code is about function: anything loose, exposed or dangling can be grabbed, burned or cut.

  • Closed-toe shoes: dropped tools and hot parts hurt
  • Long trousers: protect legs from chips and sparks
  • Nothing loose: no scarves, lanyards, open jackets or dangling jewellery
  • Hair tied back: long hair and spinning tools never mix

Personal protective equipment

Grab the right gear before you start.

  • Safety glasses: saws, drills, sanders, rotary tools
  • Ear protection: loud tools and long sessions
  • Dust mask: sanding, especially MDF
  • Gloves, it depends: yes for hot or sharp handling, no near spinning blades or bits
If something goes wrong

Stop · Contain · Care · Report

1

Stop

Switch off or pause the machine. Know where the power switch and pause button are before you start.

2

Contain

Small laser flame? Keep the lid closed and pause. Know where the fire extinguisher is.

3

Care

Use the first aid kit for minor cuts and burns. Cool burns under running water.

4

Report

Tell Sunny about every incident or near miss, even small ones. That is how the lab gets safer.

Never work alone on risky tools · Never rush · Never use a machine you haven't been shown

FLUX Beamo laser cutter, step by step

A desktop CO2 laser that focuses an invisible infrared beam into a tiny, very hot spot. It can cut right through, score a thin line, or engrave images like a printer.

300 × 210mm work area
45 mmmax material height

Laser material guide

When in doubt, leave it out and ask Sunny.

✓ Go: cut and engrave

  • Plywood, MDF, balsa
  • Cast acrylic
  • Paper, cardboard
  • Veg-tanned leather
  • Felt, cotton fabric
  • Cork, laser-safe rubber

◐ Engrave only

  • Glass
  • Stone, slate, tile
  • Anodised aluminium
  • Coated or painted metal
  • Bare metal: Beamo can't cut it

✕ Never

  • PVC / vinyl: toxic chlorine gas
  • Polycarbonate: burns, discolours
  • ABS: toxic fumes, melts
  • HDPE, foam: melts, catches fire
  • Fibreglass, carbon fibre
  • Unknown material

Tutorial 02

Bandsaw, step by step

A continuous loop of toothed steel runs around two wheels and always cuts downward, pressing the work onto the table. Great for curves, straight rip cuts and stock too thick for the laser. Needs an approved request form and your professor plus Sunny present.

Furniture and repairs

Curved legs, chair backs, and repairs that give old furniture a second life.

Reclaimed wood

Resaw pallets and old beams into usable boards (SDG 12).

Laser or bandsaw?

Laser: thin, precise, detailed 2D parts. Bandsaw: thick stock, big curves, fast rough shaping.

Power tools, step by step

Pick a tool. Every power tool session needs an approved request form, supervision, and a QR log.

Quick tips for the small tools

Rotary tool

Glasses on, clamp small parts, let it reach speed before touching the work.

Heat gun

Point away from people and cords. The nozzle stays hot after you switch it off.

Hot glue gun

Always rest it on its stand. Glue stays hot long after it looks set.

Soldering iron

Use the stand, never touch the tip, work with fume extraction and wash hands after.

Tutorial 04 NEW

Disc & belt sander, step by step

Room 408's Polo MM491G is a bench combo sander: a spinning disc on the side for rounding curves and corners, and a moving belt on top for flat faces and straight edges. It's how you make a project look finished and feel safe to hold. Needs an approved request form and supervision, like all power tools.

150 mmsanding disc
4 × 36 insanding belt (≈100 × 915 mm)
370 Wmotor
0–45°tilting disc table + miter gauge

Disc = curves and corners

Round the ears on a Desk Buddy, soften box corners, shape convex curves. Always on the downward side.

Belt = flat and straight

Flatten an end, square up an edge, flush a joint, add a chamfer. Always against the work support.

Orbital or bench sander?

Orbital sander (Power tools tab): you move the tool over big surfaces. Bench sander: you move small parts to the machine for fast shaping.

Where it's used in the real world

Toys and kids' products

Rounded, splinter-free edges are a safety requirement, not decoration.

Furniture making

Shaping legs, fitting joints and finishing edges before oiling.

Product prototypes

Taking a rough bandsaw shape to a smooth, presentable model for a pitch.

Upcycling

Turning scrap wood into products that feel brand new (SDG 12).

Desk Buddy: a phone stand + pen holder with a face

A chunky cat, bear or shiba head stands at the back of a pine base. Your phone rests on the front ledge and leans on the head; pens go in the block beside it. The laser engraves the face and your name.

  • About 2.5 hours
  • Size: 160 mm wide × 90 mm deep × about 118 mm tall
  • SDG 12: made from offcuts, built to last instead of a plastic stand
  • Laser files: DeskBuddy_head_outlines.svg (print at 100% as a tracing template) and DeskBuddy_plywood.svg
